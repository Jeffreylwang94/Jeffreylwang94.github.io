Reaction–Diffusion

Two imaginary chemicals spread out and react with each other. Out of that simple rule come coral, fingerprints, cells that divide, and mazes. Click and drag to add some chemical yourself.

Wait, this is a static page?

Yes. GitHub sent your browser this one HTML file, the same file everyone else gets, and then stepped out of the picture. Everything after that happens on your own machine:

  • The whole grid lives in your graphics card's memory as a texture.
  • A small WebGL shader program updates every cell in parallel, many times per frame. The counter above shows how much work that adds up to.
  • No server, no libraries, no build step. View the page source and the whole thing is there.

The math is the Gray–Scott model. Chemical A is constantly fed in, chemical B is constantly removed, and wherever they meet, B converts A into more B. Nudging the feed and removal rates, which is what each "Pattern" does, changes the whole character of the result.
